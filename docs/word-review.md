# Word list: items for Aidan to review

130 answers: 26 four-letter (20%), 39 five-letter (30%), 34 six-letter (26%), 31 seven-letter (24%).
39% are proper nouns, 14 are pinned to an anniversary. Every fact that contains a date, a number
or a "first" was checked with a web search on 2026-10-17; the sources are in
[`fact-sources.md`](fact-sources.md).

Nothing below is known to be wrong. These are the places where sources disagree, where a claim
is a rounded or popular figure, or where the fact describes something still changing.

## Facts I am less than fully confident about

| Word | What to look at |
|---|---|
| RING | Thickness of Saturn's rings varies by source from about 10 m to 1 km. NASA says "less than 100 metres in most places", which is what the fact says. |
| RIGEL | Luminosity estimates range from about 61,500 to 363,000 times the Sun. The fact uses the safe floor: "tens of thousands of times". |
| VOID | The Boötes Void is given as 250 to 330 million light-years across. The fact says "roughly 300 million". |
| THRUST | Saturn V first-stage thrust is quoted as 7.5, 7.6 or 7.7 million pounds. The fact uses 7.6 (ASME); the Smithsonian says "nearly 7.7". |
| DWARF | "A teaspoon would weigh tonnes": published estimates run from 1 to 15 tonnes (NASA says 5.5 tons), so only "tonnes" is claimed. |
| STORM | "Watched continuously since the 1800s" (records start in 1831). Older claims that it dates from the 1660s are disputed by a 2024 study, so the fact avoids them. |
| CHIME | "Thousands of fast radio bursts": the second CHIME/FRB catalogue (January 2026) lists 4,539. "Mostly from other galaxies" is the consensus; at least one burst came from a magnetar in our own galaxy, hence "mostly". |
| TRANSIT | "Most known exoplanets were found by the transit method": about 3,700 of 5,005 confirmed planets in 2022 (~74%). |
| PLASMA | "The most common state of ordinary matter" is a widely repeated estimate (99% or more), not a precise measurement. |
| STELLAR | Worded as "most elements heavier than helium were forged inside stars or in their explosive deaths"; a few light elements come from cosmic rays instead. |
| FALCON | "Its boosters now routinely fly again" is a statement about the present; true as of October 2026. |
| ORION | Artemis II (launched 1 April 2026, splashed down 10 April) was confirmed through news coverage of the mission; the Canadian Space Agency's page would not load for me. Worth a glance since it is pinned and names Jeremy Hansen. |
| REENTRY | Plasma blackout is real, but modern spacecraft can often relay through satellites above them, so the fact says it "can briefly block" contact. |
| EQUINOX | "Day and night nearly equal everywhere": exactly equal is a popular myth (refraction makes the day a few minutes longer), hence "nearly". |
| MAVEN | NASA lost contact in December 2025 and ended the mission in June 2026; the fact is written in the past tense ("spent over 11 years at Mars"). |

## Words that may be too obscure

Each passes "a reasonably engaged space fan would recognise it", but these are the ones I would
look at first if you want to trim:

- **ANIK** (Canada's first communications satellite, 1972): Canadian and historic; younger players may not know it.
- **CHIME** (Canadian radio telescope): the word is common, the telescope less so.
- **NEOSSAT** (Canadian asteroid-hunting telescope): your suggestion; pinned to 25 February.
- **BENNU**, **MAVEN**, **SPICA**, **CARINA**, **ERIS**, **CRUX**, **TELSTAR**: known to people who follow missions or the night sky, but not household names.
- **CUPOLA**, **TRUSS**, **GIBBOUS**, **UMBRA**, **APOGEE**, **PARSEC**: real vocabulary that a casual player may only half know. All are ordinary dictionary words, so they can at least be guessed by spelling.

A pinned word appears only on its anniversary, once a year.
- **"Proper noun"** means the fact treats the answer as a name: a planet, moon, star, constellation, spacecraft, rocket, mission or program (so MOON and EARTH count, STAR and SPACE do not). Each entry in `words.json` carries `"proper": true/false`; the scheduler uses it to avoid three in a row.
- **No people as answers.** Facts mention people only where the spacecraft or event is the subject (Gagarin on Vostok 1, Glenn on Atlas, Hansen on Orion, Bell on pulsars).
